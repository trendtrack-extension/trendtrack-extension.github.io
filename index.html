TrendTrack Extension Review (2026): What It Does and Costs

The TrendTrack extension is the free Chrome add-on attached to an ecommerce research suite. Here is what the official page confirms, and what it leaves unsaid.

Where the TrendTrack extension sits

It is not a standalone product. On the vendor's own site it appears under free tools, next to a competitor analysis report, an ads finder, a winning product finder and a ROAS break-even calculator. The paid platform beside it covers shops, an ads library, advertisers, shop analytics, similar shops, email libraries, folders, a brand tracker and an MCP connection for assistants. Understanding that arrangement matters, because free browser add-ons from research vendors serve two purposes at once: they save a real click for existing customers, and they put the brand in front of people who have not paid yet. Both can be true. It just means the add-on is designed to lead somewhere, and you should decide in advance whether you want to go there.

What the official page actually states

Very little, and that is worth saying plainly. The listing describes a free Chrome extension and promises an edge over competitors, then trails off into a general phrase about the things it lets you do. No feature list, no permissions summary, no supported-store list appears in the page we read. So this review will not tell you it reveals a store's exact revenue or theme, because the source does not say so and guessing would be the same as making it up. Before installing, open the Chrome Web Store listing itself: the description, the update date, the review count and the requested permissions are all there, and they are the only authoritative answer to what the current build does.

Permissions deserve thirty seconds

Any extension that analyses the page you are viewing needs to read that page, and some read every page you visit. That is not sinister on its own, but it is worth a deliberate look rather than a reflex click. Check what the store listing requests, whether access can be limited to click-to-run rather than always-on, and whether you are comfortable with it being active while you are logged into your own store admin or ad accounts. A practical habit for anyone testing several research add-ons is to keep them in a separate browser profile used only for competitor work, which keeps them away from your production sessions and makes uninstalling a clean operation.

When a dashboard beats an add-on

Browser tools shine for opportunistic research: you are already on a store, you want context now, one click gives it to you. They are poor at the other half of the job. Comparing thirty stores, saving creative into folders, watching a competitor over six weeks or pulling data into a spreadsheet all want a real interface and an export, which is exactly what the paid suite is selling. If your work looks like the second list, the add-on will feel charming for a week and then stop being the thing you open. Decide which of the two patterns describes your week before you judge whether the free tool was disappointing or simply aimed at a different task.

Installing it sensibly

  1. Read the store listing first
    Open the Chrome Web Store page and check the last update date, the review count and the permissions requested. An add-on untouched for a year usually reflects a product that moved on.
  2. Put it in a spare profile
    Create a browser profile used only for competitor research and install it there. Your ad accounts and store admin stay in the profile that has no research add-ons at all.
  3. Test it on a store you know
    Open a shop whose real numbers you have seen and compare what the overlay reports. Estimates that miss badly on a known store will miss on unknown ones too.
  4. Handle ads somewhere else
    When the question turns into which creative a rival is running and who is promoting them, move to Reachara and keep the add-on for quick context on individual stores.

Quick answers

Is the TrendTrack extension free?

The vendor lists it under free tools and describes it as a free Chrome extension, so there is no charge to install. Whether every feature stays usable without an account on the paid platform is not stated on that page, which is a common arrangement worth confirming in the store listing before you rely on it.

What data does the extension show?

The official listing does not enumerate it, and we will not invent a list. The Chrome Web Store description for the current build is the authoritative source. Expect the kind of store-level context the paid platform reports, shown for whichever page you are viewing, rather than the full research suite.

Does it work in browsers other than Chrome?

The vendor describes a Chrome extension. Chromium-based browsers such as Edge, Brave and Arc often accept Chrome extensions, but that is a property of those browsers rather than a promise from the vendor. Firefox and Safari use different extension formats, so check the store listing if you need either.

Is it safe to install a research extension?

The risk is not malware so much as reach: these tools read the pages you browse. Review the requested permissions, prefer click-to-run access if offered, and keep research add-ons in a browser profile separate from the one holding your ad accounts and store admin sessions.

Do I still need the paid platform?

For opportunistic lookups, no. For comparing many stores, saving creative, tracking a competitor over weeks or exporting anything, yes, because an overlay cannot do those. Work out which of those two patterns matches your week, then decide whether the subscription earns its place.
